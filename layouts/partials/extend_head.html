{{/* ANALYTICS A pageview is sent only once the visitor shows a sign of life: a pointer, key, wheel or touch event the browser raised itself (isTrusted), or leaving the page (hidden, pagehide) after it was visible. Crawlers that render the page in a real browser and kill it once the network is idle do neither and are never counted. In October/November 2026 one on Chinese home connections was more than half of the visitors of lna-dev.net; its clean headers got past every filter in front of Plausible (../Web-Services, caddy/Caddyfile, muninn.lna-dev.net). A real visitor leaves the page at some point, and Plausible's engagement event needs the same: in the logs over 99 % of real pageviews had one and the crawler's had none. Scroll is no sign of life: a deep link to an anchor scrolls on load. Nor is a pointermove without movement: after that scroll, and whenever the layout shifts under the cursor, Chrome raises one itself, isTrusted and with movementX/Y 0, also in a crawler nobody steers (tried with Playwright on #download, headless and headed). Costs: the realtime view shows a visitor on the first interaction rather than on load. Time on page starts at the pageview, so the seconds before the first interaction are missing. A visitor counted only on leaving gets no time on page at all. The tracker (v33) starts its clock when the pageview is sent, and only then adds the visibilitychange/blur listeners that send the engagement event. A listener added while visibilitychange is being dispatched does not run for that event, and its clock would read about 0 anyway. transformRequest never sees the engagement event, so the time measured here cannot be handed over. A possible fix, not done: send our own engagement event after the pageview, with the visible time measured here. It copies the tracker's private payload (n, sd, e, v: 33), which an update can break silently. Both requests also leave while the page closes, so the engagement event may reach Plausible before its pageview and be dropped; that needs trying against the server. Measure first: after this change, real pageviews without an engagement event are exactly this group, since the crawler is no longer counted at all. muninn's request_body in the access log shows them. A page restored from the back/forward cache counts again, as Plausible's own capture does. autoCapturePageviews loses nothing else: every pushState on the site (lightbox, story/grid, footer) changes only the hash, which Plausible ignores. Events raised before the pageview are held and sent after it, or a crawler would start a visit with them: lightbox.js reports "Image View" when a deep link opens a photo on load.
